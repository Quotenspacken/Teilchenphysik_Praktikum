\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:Discussion}
The measured global CP asymmetry,
\begin{equation*}
    A_{CP} = 0.049 \pm 0.007,
\end{equation*}
deviates from zero by $6.3$ standard deviations, 
constituting strong evidence for CP violation in the decay $B^{\pm} \to K^{\pm}K^{+}K^{-}$.

The Dalitz plot reveals a prominent horizontal band at $m^2 \approx 1.17 \times 10^7\,\text{MeV}^2$,
in good agreement with the squared mass of the $\chi_{c0}$ charmonium state,
$m^2_{\chi_{c0}} = (3414.71\,\text{MeV})^2 \approx 1.166 \times 10^7\,\text{MeV}^2$ \cite{PDG2024}.
The $\chi_{c0}$ can decay into a $K^+K^-$ pair, making it an expected intermediate resonance in the $B^{\pm} \to K^{\pm}K^+K^-$ decay.
Its removal prior to the local asymmetry study prevents this resonance from biasing the phase-space-dependent CP asymmetry measurement.
A second relevant band is observed at $m^2 \approx 9.56 \times 10^6\,\text{MeV}^2$,
in good agreement with the squared mass of the $J/\psi(1s)$ charmonium state\cite{PDG2024}.

After removal of the $\chi_{c0}$ contribution, a region of significant local CP violation is identified at low values of $m^2(K^+K^-)$,
specifically in the range $m^2(K^+K^-)_1 < 0.5 \times 10^7\,\text{MeV}^2$ and $0.5 \times 10^7 < m^2(K^+K^-)_2 < 1.7 \times 10^7\,\text{MeV}^2$.
In this region, the $B^+$ yield clearly exceeds the $B^-$ yield,
corresponding to a positive local asymmetry.
This finding is consistent with the observation by LHCb that the CP asymmetry in $B^{\pm} \to K^{\pm}K^+K^-$ is not uniformly distributed across phase space but is concentrated in specific regions of the Dalitz plot,
particularly at low $K^+K^-$ invariant mass where contributions from broad scalar resonances and their interference with non-resonant amplitudes are expected.

The coarse binning adopted in this analysis limits the precision with which the exact boundaries and magnitude of the asymmetric region can be determined.
A finer binning, or preferably a full amplitude fit, would be needed to resolve the underlying dynamics.
Furthermore, the bin-by-bin significance is not corrected for the look-elsewhere effect:
since many bins are tested simultaneously, some may appear significant by chance.
The observed clustering of significant bins in a contiguous phase-space region, however, reduces this concern.
