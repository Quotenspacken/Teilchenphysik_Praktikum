\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:Discussion}
While the presented analysis yields a statistically significant $B_s$ signal, 
several simplifications and limitations should be noted.

The thresholds used in the feature selection (Kolmogorov-Smirnov 
statistic of $0.6$ for simulation/data agreement, $0.5$ for separation 
power, and a correlation of $0.8$ with the invariant mass) were chosen 
rather loosely. Stricter cuts would have removed too many features, 
leaving an insufficient number for a stable BDT training. As a result, 
the selection should be understood as a pragmatic compromise rather 
than a rigorously optimized choice, and the stability of the classifier 
with respect to these thresholds was not systematically studied.

The significance proxy $m$ used here is a simplified estimator and 
does not correspond to a rigorous statistical treatment, such as one 
based on profile likelihoods or toy Monte Carlo studies. It also does 
not account for systematic uncertainties, e.g.\ from the choice of fit 
model, the sideband-based background estimation, or the simulation 
samples used to fix the peak shapes. In particular, the assumption 
that the sideband region above the $B_s$ mass accurately represents 
the combinatorial background under the signal peaks was not validated.

Finally, the relatively low number of signal events ($n_{\text{sig}} 
\approx 51$) limits the statistical precision of the result and makes 
the fit sensitive to the exact definition of the signal region. A more 
robust treatment would involve varying the signal window and assessing 
the resulting stability of $n_{\text{sig}}$, $n_{\text{bkg}}$, and $m$.
